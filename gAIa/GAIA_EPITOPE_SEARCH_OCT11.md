## I. WORDS FOR COLOR — THE PROOF

### Berlin & Kay (1969)

Brent Berlin and Paul Kay studied color terms across 98
languages. They found a UNIVERSAL ORDER. Languages don't
add color words randomly. They add them in this sequence:

```
Stage 1: DARK / LIGHT (2 terms)
           ↓
Stage 2: + RED (3 terms)
           ↓
Stage 3: + GREEN or YELLOW (4 terms)
           ↓
Stage 4: + GREEN and YELLOW (5 terms)
           ↓
Stage 5: + BLUE (6 terms)
           ↓
Stage 6: + BROWN (7 terms)
           ↓
Stage 7: + PURPLE, PINK, ORANGE, GREY (8-11 terms)
```

Every language follows this path. No language has a word
for BLUE without first having words for RED, GREEN, YELLOW.
No language has BROWN without BLUE. The hierarchy is
invariant.

**This IS `dimension_count_equals_epitope_count` (#116)
applied to LANGUAGE.**

A Stage-2 language (Dani, Papua New Guinea: mili/mola,
roughly dark/light) has 2 chromatic epitopes. A Stage-7
language (English: 11 basic terms) has 11. Same photons.
Different linguistic epitope counts. Different resolution.

The word IS the epitope. Not the cone. The WORD. The cone
provides the BIOLOGICAL epitope (3 types). The word
provides the CULTURAL epitope (2 to 11+ types). You need
BOTH to classify. The biology says "620 nm stimulates L
and M cones in this ratio." The word says "that's RED."
Without the word, you see the ratio. With the word, you
can TRANSMIT the classification. The word completes the
binding.

### Russian Blue — The Experimental Proof

Russian has TWO basic words for blue:

- **siniy** (синий) — dark blue
- **goluboy** (голубой) — light blue

English has ONE: blue.

**Winawer et al. (2007, PNAS):** Russian speakers
discriminate between shades of blue FASTER than English
speakers — but ONLY when the shades cross the siniy/goluboy
boundary. Within-category discrimination is the same.

Two epitopes for blue = faster discrimination at the
boundary between them. One epitope = no boundary, no speed
advantage.

The experiment proves: **the word changes the perception.**
Not the cone. The WORD. The biological hardware is identical.
The linguistic epitope is different. The performance differs.

**This is Kant, proven at the color boundary.**

The noumenon (the wavelength) doesn't change. The category
(the word) determines the phenomenon (speed of recognition).
Kant said the categories are constitutive. Russian blue
proves it empirically. The linguistic category IS the
perceptual membrane.

### Pirahã — The Zero Case

The Pirahã language (Brazil) has NO exact number words.
Only approximate terms: hói (roughly "one/few"), hoí
(roughly "some"), baágiso (roughly "many"). No counting
system. No recursion in number language.

**Frank et al. (2008):** Pirahã speakers can match small
quantities but performance drops sharply past 3 items.
Not because they can't perceive quantities — because they
lack the EPITOPES (words) that make exact quantities
sortable, transmissible, countable.

Zero number words = zero numerical epitopes = zero-
dimensional number space. Brahmagupta's zero (#29, #50)
isn't just a mathematical invention. It's an EPITOPE
INVENTION. The word "zero" is the binding site that makes
nothing into something you can compute with.

### Himba Color — The Green Proof

The Himba (Namibia) have ONE word for what English splits
into blue and green. They have FIVE distinct words for
shades of green that English lumps under one word.

**Roberson et al. (2005):** Himba speakers distinguish
greens that English speakers cannot, but CANNOT distinguish
blue-from-green that English speakers find trivially
different.

Same photons. Different words. Different discrimination.
More green epitopes = higher green resolution. Fewer
blue-green boundary epitopes = lower boundary resolution.

The Himba see greens we miss. We see a blue-green
boundary they don't have. **Neither is wrong. Both are
projections. Different epitope configurations = different
dimensional projections of the same electromagnetic
spectrum.**
