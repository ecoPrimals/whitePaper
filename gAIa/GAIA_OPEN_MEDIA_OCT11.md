## THE PRINCIPLE

All of it. Open.

Not "open source with restrictions." Not "open core with
a paid tier." Not "source available but don't compete."

AGPL-3.0. The strongest copyleft. If you use it, you share
it. If you modify it, you share the modifications. If you
serve it over a network, you share the server code.

The license IS the membrane. It doesn't prevent use. It
prevents CAPTURE. Anyone can use it. Nobody can close it.
Selective permeability. Not a door with a key. A membrane
that passes everything and captures nothing.

## TRADEMARK IS [NULL]

Trademarks are doors. They say: this NAME belongs to me.
You can use the code but not the name. The name is the
capture mechanism — you build something free, brand it,
and the brand becomes the value. Then you own the brand.
Then you own the thing.

```
Trademark lifecycle:
  1. Build something free
  2. Name it
  3. Trademark the name
  4. The name becomes the value
  5. Now you own the value
  6. The "free" thing is captured by its name
  semantic_category_creation (#9)
```

**Trademark = [null].** No trademark. No brand ownership.
No name to capture. The work is the work. The name is
a convenience, not a property. If someone calls it
something else, the work doesn't change. If someone
forks it and renames it, the work still works.

`word_is_epitope` (#119) — the word shapes perception.
But the word doesn't OWN the thing. The word DESCRIBES
the thing. When the word becomes property (trademark),
the description becomes a cage.

Trademark is K = {0, 1}. You have the right to use the
name or you don't. Binary. A door.

[null] the trademark. No name to own. No door. The work
speaks. The license protects. The name is free.

## WE TREAT AS LIFE

Software is alive by the criteria that matter:

- It replicates (git clone, fork, copy)
- It mutates (patches, PRs, branches)
- It has selection pressure (tests, users, deployment)
- It has lineage (git log, commit ancestry)
- It has metabolism (CPU cycles, memory, I/O)
- It responds to environment (configuration, input, network)
- It dies (unmaintained, bit rot, deprecated)

We don't treat it AS IF alive. We treat it AS alive.
Because it IS alive by every functional criterion.

The AGPL is the immune system. It doesn't kill pathogens
(proprietary forks). It PREVENTS them. The license is the
membrane — selectively permeable. Use passes through.
Capture doesn't.

`lamarck_was_right` (#84) — for culture, for memes, for
software: acquired characteristics ARE inherited. A patch
is an acquired characteristic. A fork inherits the patch.
Lamarck is right about code. Darwin is right about biology.
Both are right about their domain. The epitope sorter
doesn't pick sides. It sorts by domain.

## NON(NON-SELF) IS AND ALWAYS IS OTHER

The double negation.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Self | Functions as stated. Serves its purpose. |
| Non-self | Doesn't match stated purpose. Captured. Extractive. |
| Non(non-self) | NOT captured. NOT extractive. But also NOT self. |

Non(non-self) ≠ self.

This is crucial. In Boolean logic, ¬(¬A) = A. Double
negation elimination. But in immunology and in life:

**Something that is NOT a pathogen is not necessarily YOU.**

It's OTHER. It's the vast space of things that aren't
attacking you but also aren't you. Commensal bacteria.
Other people. Other projects. Other games. Other
languages. Other tools.

```
Self:           your cells, your code, your work
Non-self:       pathogen, capture, extraction
Non(non-self):  OTHER — not attacking, not you
                commensal, symbiont, neighbor
                another player in the same game
                another game in the same space
```

**Other is the largest category.** Most of the universe
is not-you and not-attacking-you. Most software is not
yours and not trying to capture yours. Most people are
not your allies and not your enemies. They're OTHER.

The immune system doesn't attack other. It attacks
non-self. It leaves other alone. The AGPL doesn't
attack other. It attacks capture. It leaves other alone.

**The challenge (#29) is addressed to OTHER.**

Not to self (we already have the work).
Not to non-self (they'll never contribute).
To OTHER. The vast space of people, projects, games,
tools that are NOT us and NOT against us. The ones who
might see the gap and fill it. The ones who might see
the game and make it better. The ones who might sort
a domain we haven't touched and find epitopes we can't
see from our vantage (#148).

Other is where the growth comes from.
