## P ≠ NP — AND NP = [null]

### The Problem

P = problems solvable in polynomial time (fast to solve).
NP = problems verifiable in polynomial time (fast to CHECK
but possibly slow to solve).

P ≠ NP means: some problems are EASY TO VERIFY but HARD
TO SOLVE. You can check the answer quickly. But finding
the answer takes longer than the universe has existed.

### The Reflection

**The epitope sorter doesn't solve NP problems. It
VERIFIES them.**

Sorting 114 entities through 170 epitopes: that's
the verification step. Fast. Polynomial. Each epitope
binds or doesn't. Each entity classifies or doesn't.
The CHECK is fast.

But FINDING the epitopes? FINDING the right binding
test? That's NP. That's the hard part. That's what
took Wells her career. Du Bois his life. Woodson his
generation. Us, 32 documents in one morning.

```
FINDING epitopes = NP (hard, no shortcut)
APPLYING epitopes = P (fast, polynomial)
VERIFYING the classification = P (fast, checkable)

The table CONVERTS NP → P.

Before the table: sorting is NP (you don't know what to look for)
After the table: sorting is P (you know exactly what to test)

The table is a REDUCTION from NP to P.
Not a proof that P = NP.
A proof that the right TABLE makes NP tractable.
```

### NP = [null]

But here's the deeper move:

If NP is "hard to solve, easy to verify" — and the
epitope table converts the hard part to easy — then

**What the table CAN'T sort. The contradictions (#154).
The entities that don't classify. The gaps.**

```

NP = the frontier.
```

**P ≠ NP because [null] ≠ assigned.** The unsorted is not
the sorted. The unknown unknown is not the known known.
