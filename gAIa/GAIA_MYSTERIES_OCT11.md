## 1. THE ANTIKYTHERA MECHANISM — A Computer With No Manual

**What it is:** A hand-powered bronze astronomical calculator.
82 surviving fragments. ~30 corroded gearwheels. Shoebox-sized.

**When:** ~100 BCE. Built in the Hellenistic world. Lost in a
Roman shipwreck off Antikythera, Greece.

**Found:** 1901 by sponge divers. Recognized as mechanical in 1902.

**What it does (now understood):**
- Tracks Sun and Moon positions in the zodiac
- Predicts lunar phases
- Predicts solar and lunar eclipses (Saros cycle, 223 months)
- Tracks the 19-year Metonic cycle
- Tracks the 4-year Olympic games cycle
- Epicyclic gearing models the Moon's elliptical orbit
- Probably displayed all 5 known planets (subsystem lost)

**What's mysterious:**
Not what it does — we've decoded most of that. What's mysterious
is that nothing this complex existed again for **over 1,000 years.**
It combines Babylonian astronomical cycles, Plato's Academy
mathematics, and Hipparchos's lunar theory. It was built, used,
lost, and forgotten. No tradition carried it forward. The knowledge
died with the shipwreck.

**Epitope #213 — dead_end_technology**
The Antikythera Mechanism is a technological dead end. It existed.
It was sophisticated. It was lost. No lineage connects it to
medieval clockwork. The gap between 100 BCE and comparable devices
is over a millennium. Some technologies die. The mechanism is proof
that progress is not linear. Capability can be lost. Knowledge can
drown.

## 2. THE ROMAN DODECAHEDRA — 100+ Objects, Zero Explanations

**What they are:** Hollow bronze objects, 12 pentagonal faces,
knobs at each corner, circular holes of varying sizes in each
face. 4-11 cm across. No two identical.

**When:** 2nd-4th century CE.

**Where:** 100+ found across northern Europe (Britain, France,
Germany, Netherlands). Almost none in the Mediterranean.

**What's mysterious:**
- **50+ proposed explanations.** Surveying tool. Candle holder.
  Dice. Pipe gauge. Sowing calendar. Glove-knitting frame.
  Scepter decoration. Children's toy. Religious object.
- **No contemporary mention.** Not in ANY Roman text. Not in
  ANY Roman image. For a culture that documented everything,
  these objects are completely invisible in the literary record.
- **Also found:** A Roman ICOSAHEDRON (d20), misclassified as a
  dodecahedron for years, stored in a museum basement.
- **Also found:** Ancient Ptolemaic/Roman d20s in faience with
  Greek letters — possibly for divination with oracle books
  (the Homeromanteion). Met Museum holds one from Egypt.
- **Also found:** A solid SILVER dodecahedron from Geneva (1982)
  with zodiac signs on each face.

**The Arloff Icosahedron:** Found near Arloff, Germany. Hollow
bronze. Spheres on corners. Two opposite faces have holes. Three
knobs around one face are larger — giving it an orientation. If
the large knobs are feet, the bigger hole faces UP.

Nobody knows what it is. It's in the Rheinisches Landesmuseum
in Bonn. You can look at it. You cannot explain it.

**Epitope #214 — artifact_without_context**
An object exists. It was clearly manufactured with skill and
purpose. It costs significant resources (bronze was expensive).
But no text, no image, no instruction survives. The artifact
exists in a cultural void. The OBJECT is the only record. This
is the inverse of zk-proof: the proof exists, but the STATEMENT
it proves is lost. You can verify it was made. You cannot verify
what it means.

**Epitope #215 — ancient_d20**
The Ptolemaic/Roman d20 at the Met (2nd c. BCE - 4th c. CE) has
Greek letters on each face. Possibly used with oracle books —
throw the die, look up the answer. Divination as algorithm:
input (random throw) → lookup table → output (prophecy). This
is a database query. The d20 is the random seed. The oracle
book is the response table. The ritual is the API.

## 3. THE PHAISTOS DISC — The World's Only Copy

**What it is:** A fired clay disc, 16 cm diameter, covered on
both sides with 241 stamped symbols in spiral arrangement.
45 distinct signs. 61 groups (words?). Stamped BEFORE firing.

**When:** ~1700-1650 BCE (Middle Minoan).

**Where:** Palace of Phaistos, Crete.

**Found:** July 3, 1908 by Luigi Pernier.

**What's mysterious:**
- **45 stamps.** Someone CARVED 45 individual stamp tools to
  make this disc. That implies an intention to make MORE.
  But no other disc has ever been found.
- **The script is unique.** No other inscription in this script
  exists anywhere. Linear A (also undeciphered) and Linear B
  (deciphered) are different systems.
- **Repeating groups** suggest a hymn or incantation. The
  repetition is like a refrain.
- **One object, one script, one copy.** You can't decipher a
  language from a single text with no parallel. There is
  not enough data.

**Epitope #216 — insufficient_data**
The Phaistos Disc cannot be deciphered because there is only
one. A single text in an unknown script provides insufficient
data for decipherment. The information is THERE — stamped in
clay, fired, preserved for 3,700 years. But without a parallel
text, a bilingual inscription, or more examples, the key is
permanently lost. This is the information-theoretic limit of
single samples. n=1 = [null].

## 4. THE VOYNICH MANUSCRIPT — 600 Years of Unbroken Cipher

**What it is:** An illustrated codex, 240 pages, hand-written in
an undeciphered script ("Voynichese"). 113 unidentified plant
species. Astronomical diagrams. Bathing scenes. Pharmaceutical
drawings.

**When:** Vellum carbon-dated to 1404-1438.

**Where:** Probably Central Europe or possibly Colonial New Spain.

**What's mysterious:**
- **The text has statistical properties of natural language.**
  Word distribution is consistent with real language, not random.
  But no known language matches.
- **WWI and WWII codebreakers failed.** William Friedman (who
  broke Japanese PURPLE cipher), Elizebeth Friedman, Prescott
  Currier, John Tiltman — all tried, all failed.
- **The plants are composites.** Roots from one species, leaves
  from another, flowers from a third. Either they're imaginary,
  or they're an encoding system, or they're drawn from memory
  by someone who saw them but didn't have them in front of them.
- **Some plants may be New World species.** If confirmed, the
  manuscript can't predate 1493 (Columbus). But the vellum
  dates to 1404-1438. The vellum could be older than the text.

**Epitope #217 — unbroken_cipher**
The best codebreakers in history — the people who broke Enigma,
PURPLE, and every wartime cipher — could not break the Voynich.
Either it's a hoax (but the statistics say language), or it uses
a method we haven't conceived. Some ciphers resist ALL keys.
Some systems are designed to be readable only by the maker. The
Voynich is a membrane with no known receptor. The binding site
exists. No epitope matches.

## 5. THE MAYA LONG COUNT — A Calendar Smarter Than Its Interpreters

**What it is:** A continuous day-count calendar starting August 11,
3114 BCE. Base-20 notation. Cycles: kin (1 day), uinal (20),
tun (360), katun (7,200), baktun (144,000).

**What's NOT mysterious:**
- The 2012 "apocalypse" was modern nonsense. The Maya never
  predicted it. The calendar rolled over from 13.0.0.0.0 to
  14.0.0.0.0. Like an odometer hitting 100000.
- Wandering Wolf (Mayan Grand Elder, 2011): "2012 is not the
  end of the world, nor did we ever predict that it would end."

**What IS mysterious:**
- **Eclipse prediction accuracy.** The Dresden Codex eclipse
  table predicted ALL 77 solar eclipses between 755-788 CE
  within 3 days. Using no telescopes. Using no mechanized
  instruments. Pure mathematical computation encoded in bark
  paper.
- **Recyclable tables.** The eclipse tables include instructions
  for recycling the predictions across 1,400 years. Tested
  against Western astronomical data — they work.
- **No known transmission.** Maya astronomy developed
  independently of Greek, Babylonian, Indian, or Chinese
  systems. Convergent evolution of precision.

**Epitope #218 — convergent_precision**
The Maya, Babylonians, Greeks, Indians, and Aboriginal
Australians all independently developed precise astronomical
systems. They couldn't have communicated (separated by oceans,
millennia, and language). Yet they converged on the same
observations. The sky is the same. The math is the same. The
precision is the same. Convergent evolution of knowledge proves
the knowledge is in the PHENOMENON, not the observer. The tool
discovers what was always there.

## 6. THE "BAGHDAD BATTERY" — The Mystery That Wasn't

**What it is:** A ceramic jar containing a copper cylinder sealed
with bitumen, with an iron rod inside. Found at Khujut Rabu,
near Baghdad.

**When:** 3rd c. BCE - 3rd c. CE (Parthian or Sasanian).

**The narrative:** Wilhelm König (1938) proposed it was a galvanic
cell for electroplating. The theory went viral. It's in textbooks.
It's in documentaries. It's called "evidence of ancient
electricity."

**The reality:** 
- No ancient Mesopotamian object has ever been shown to be
  electroplated. The gilded pieces König cited were mercury-gilded.
- Paul Craddock (British Museum): "There's never been any
  irrefutable evidence to support the electroplating theory."
- The craftsmen's technique König observed in Baghdad silversmiths
  was traced by Gerhard Eggert to a process patented by Elkington
  in Birmingham in 1840. It had traveled TO Baghdad in the 19th
  century, not FROM antiquity.
- The jar was found alongside incantation bowls — ritual
  containers for prayers, curses, or spells. The iron "rod" may
  have been a nail for sealing.

**König read the past out of what was actually the recent present.**

**Epitope #219 — false_mystery**
The Baghdad Battery is a mystery manufactured by misinterpretation.
The artifact is real. The narrative is fabricated. König projected
modern technology backward into ancient objects. The gilding was
mercury. The technique was Victorian. The jar was ritual. This is
the inverse of the Phaistos Disc: the OBJECT is understood, but
the STORY about it is false. Not all mysteries are genuine gaps.
Some are projection. The epitope sorter must distinguish between
[null] (genuine absence) and false positive (projected meaning).
