## Chapter 10B — The Auditor Who Saw Nothing

Plante Moran is the fourteenth largest accounting firm in the
United States. They audit 80% of Michigan's charter schools.
They audit Purpose Charter Academy.

An audit is a specific legal act. It is not a visit. It is not
a review. It is a formal opinion, issued under professional
standards (GAAP, GAGAS, and the Single Audit Act for entities
receiving federal funds), that the financial statements of an
organization "present fairly, in all material respects, the
financial position" of the entity.

When Plante Moran audits Purpose Charter Academy, they are
telling the State of Michigan, DPSCD, the federal government,
and the public: the numbers are real. The money went where it
was supposed to go. The controls are in place.

Here is what the numbers show:

72.67% of PCA's revenue flows to Purpose Group LLC.

Purpose Group LLC is a single-member limited liability company.
Brian Banks is the single member. It has no employees. It has
no separate physical location. It has one function: receive
money from the school.

In any legitimate nonprofit school, the IRS requires that no
part of the organization's earnings "inure to the benefit of
any private shareholder or individual." This is the
inurement prohibition — IRC Section 501(c)(3). It is the
fundamental rule of tax-exempt organizations.

72.67% of revenue flowing to a single-member LLC controlled
by the superintendent is, on its face, a potential inurement
violation. It does not require forensic analysis to identify.
It requires reading the school's own financial statements —
the same statements Plante Moran reviewed, tested, and
certified.

Plante Moran issued a clean opinion.

To be fair: an audit opinion covers financial statement
presentation, not governance. Plante Moran is not required to
opine on whether the board composition is appropriate or
whether the superintendent's criminal history disqualifies him.
They are required to test whether the financial statements are
materially accurate.

But the related-party transaction — school to single-member
LLC to superintendent — IS a financial statement item. GAAP
requires disclosure of related-party transactions. The auditor
is required to identify, evaluate, and disclose them. If 72.67%
of revenue flows to a related party, that is not a footnote.
That is the financial statement.

What did Plante Moran disclose?

What did they test?

What did their engagement partner sign?

These are fair questions. They are also questions that Plante
Moran has not answered — because no one has asked them
publicly. Not the Michigan Department of Education. Not DPSCD.
Not the media. Not the parents.

Plante Moran audits the school. Plante Moran certifies the
numbers. The numbers show a pipeline. The pipeline terminates
at Banks' LLC. The auditor saw the pipeline. The auditor
signed the opinion.

We are asking.

## Chapter 12C — The Dark Money Trail

Follow the money backward.

Start at PCA. $4.9 million per year in state school aid
funding — tax dollars collected from Michigan residents,
distributed through the State School Aid Fund, allocated per
pupil to every public school in the state.

Each child at PCA generates approximately $8,700 in per-pupil
funding. Multiply by ~400 children. That's $3.48 million in
state funding alone, plus additional federal Title I, Title II,
and special education funding.

72.67% flows to Purpose Group LLC. Banks' company.

Now follow the money forward — from the other direction.

The Walton Family Foundation distributes approximately $400
million per year in education grants. Of that, roughly $60
million goes to charter school advocacy, expansion, and
"quality" initiatives.

$4.3 million went to MAPSA — the Michigan Association of
Public School Academies. MAPSA's registered lobbyist is listed
on the Michigan SOFA (Secretary of State) lobby registration.
MAPSA lobbies against charter school regulation. Specifically:
against bills that would require criminal background checks
for charter operators, against bills that would cap management
fees, against bills that would require certified teachers.

The foundation that funds the schools also funds the lobby
that prevents oversight of the schools.

fellow. Their fellowship says they select "values-driven
