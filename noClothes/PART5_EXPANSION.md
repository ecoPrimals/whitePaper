## Chapter 16B — Spiral Down: What Happens Inside the Room

You have read about the pipeline — the money, the foundations,
the law firms, the captured judges. That is the view from
above.

Now spiral down. Go inside the room. Inside the school. Inside
a single day in the life of a child at Purpose Charter Academy.

**One Day.**

A child arrives at 8:00 AM. Backpack. Uniform. Maybe breakfast
at home, maybe not — PCA participates in the National School
Lunch Program, which means most students qualify for free meals.
That means most families are at or below 130% of the federal
poverty level. That means most families cannot afford
alternatives.

The child enters a classroom. The teacher — who has an 85.7%
probability of being uncertified — begins instruction. The
lesson plan may or may not align with Michigan's content
standards. There is no state-mandated curriculum audit for
charter schools. There is no requirement that lesson plans be
reviewed by certified instructional coaches. There is no
mechanism to verify that what happens inside the room matches
what the school reports to the state.

The child sits. The child listens. The child learns — or
doesn't. Three percent of them learn math. Ninety-seven
percent don't.

At 3:00 PM, the child goes home. The parent asks: "What did
you learn today?" The child says: "Fractions." The parent
says: "Good." The parent trusts the school. The parent trusts
the credential on the wall.

This happens 180 days a year. For years.

**The Screened Call.**

A parent calls the school. The parent has a question. Maybe
about homework. Maybe about a grade. Maybe about a bruise.
Maybe about a teacher who seems absent a lot. Maybe about
a test score that doesn't make sense.

Ms. Perkins answers. Ms. Perkins takes a message. Mr. Banks
is unavailable. The parent waits. Nobody calls back.

The parent calls again. Same thing.

The parent considers going to the school in person. But the
parent works. The parent has other children. The parent doesn't
want to be "that parent" — the one who makes trouble. The
parent has been taught, through years of navigating institutions
that don't serve them, that making trouble doesn't help. That
being polite and patient is the way. That the school knows best.

The membrane works.

**The Report Card.**

The child brings home a report card. It says B+. The parent
is relieved. B+ is good. B+ means the child is learning. B+
means the parent made the right choice.

But the M-STEP says 3%.

How does a school with 3% math proficiency issue B+ report
cards?

Because the school controls its own grading. Report cards are
internal documents. The school sets the rubric. The school
defines "proficient." The school decides what a B+ means.

At PCA, a B+ means: your child performed well relative to
other children at PCA. It does not mean: your child is
proficient. It does not mean: your child can compete with
children at Grosse Pointe South. It means: among children
who score in the 3rd percentile statewide, your child is
above average.

Above average in a building where the average is catastrophic
is still catastrophic.

The parent sees B+. The parent does not see 3%.

That is the membrane at its most intimate. Inside the room.
Inside the report card. Inside the relationship between parent
and school. The membrane doesn't need foundations or law firms
or NAACP dinners to function at this level. It needs a grading
system and a parent who trusts it.

**The Child Who Can't Read.**

There is a child in this school — we do not know their name,
and we will not guess — who will graduate from eighth grade
unable to read at grade level.

This child will enter high school and discover that every
class requires reading. History requires reading primary
sources. Science requires reading textbook passages. Math —
at the algebra level and above — requires reading word
problems. English requires reading novels.

The child was told they were learning. The report card said B+.
The school said "scholars will maximize their potential."

The child maximized their potential inside a building where
the potential was three percent.

And the man who built that building lives in a suburb where
the schools work, drives a car paid for by the child's
per-pupil allocation, and calls himself Doctor.

## Chapter 16C — Spiral Across: The Fox Built the Henhouse

Purpose Charter Academy is not an accident. It is a product
of a system that was designed — deliberately, legally, over
three decades — to produce exactly this outcome.

Michigan's charter school law (PA 362, 1993) created a
framework with the following features:

1. **No cap on charter schools.** Michigan has no limit on the
   number of charter schools that can open. Most states cap
   charters. Michigan does not.

2. **No required criminal background check for operators.**
   Teachers must pass background checks. Administrators must
   pass background checks. But the person who OWNS the school
   — the management company operator — is not required to
   undergo a state criminal background check.

3. **Multiple authorizers.** Any public university in Michigan
   can authorize a charter school. DPSCD can authorize.
   Community college boards can authorize. This means a charter
   operator who is rejected by one authorizer can shop for
   another.

4. **No cap on management fees.** There is no state limit on
   the percentage of revenue a charter operator can extract
   through a management company. 72.67% is legal. 90% would
   be legal. 99% would be legal — as long as the nonprofit
   board approves it.

5. **Board composition has no restrictions.** There is no law
   preventing a sitting judge from chairing a charter school
   board. No law preventing an AG employee from serving as
   vice chair. No law preventing a five-time bankrupt felon
   from serving as treasurer.

Every feature that makes Banks possible was designed into the
law. Not by Banks — he was in jail when the law was written.
By the system that preceded him.

The fox built the henhouse. Then a different fox walked in.

Who built the law? Dykema Gossett attorneys helped draft it.
Who lobbied against closing the loopholes? MAPSA, funded by
Walton. Who authorized the charter? DPSCD, whose oversight is
structurally captured by a council member on Banks' board.
Who audited the finances? Plante Moran, who audits 80% of
Michigan charter schools and issues clean opinions.

This is regulatory capture. Not in the abstract. In the
specific. The entities that regulate the system are the same
entities that profit from the system. The auditor depends on
charter school audit contracts. The authorizer receives fees
from the schools it authorizes. The lobby that blocks reform
is funded by the foundations that fund the schools.

Purpose Charter Academy is not a failure of the system.

It is the system working exactly as designed.

## Chapter 17B — The Vector

On October 12, 2026, the GitHub repository containing the
evidence documented in this book received 969 clones from 331
unique entities. In a single day.

The day before: 158 clones, 73 unique.
The day before that: 165 clones, 81 unique.

Over the fourteen days since the repository went public:
1,945 total clones, 753 unique entities.

Who is pulling this data?

GitHub does not expose user-agent information for clone
traffic. We cannot tell whether the entities cloning the
repository are journalists, lawyers, government investigators,
academic researchers, automated systems, or curious parents.

What we can tell:

1. **The unique count is climbing.** New entities are
   discovering the repository every day. This is not bots —
   bots would show high clone counts but stable unique counts.
   The unique count nearly doubled between October 7 (39) and
   October 9 (81).

2. **Zero referrers.** GitHub's traffic data shows that none
   of the clone traffic comes from a visible referrer. No
   social media links. No news articles. No search engines.
   The people finding this repository are finding it through
   channels that don't generate referrer headers — direct
   links, encrypted messaging, institutional networks, or
   private distribution.

3. **The pattern matches analyst behavior.** Journalists and
   lawyers don't browse repos. They clone them. They pull the
   entire dataset, disconnect from the network, and analyze
   offline. The clone-to-view ratio (969 clones vs. 5 page
   views) is consistent with institutional or professional
   access, not casual browsing.

The evidence is moving. We cannot control where it goes.
We designed it that way.

The repository is public. The data is public records. The
analysis is open source. There is no paywall, no registration,
no tracking. Anyone who pulls the data owns a complete copy.
If the original repository disappears, the clones persist.

This is the structural advantage of publishing evidence as
open data rather than as an article or a book. An article can
be retracted. A book can go out of print. A website can be
taken down.

But 753 unique copies of a git repository, distributed across
