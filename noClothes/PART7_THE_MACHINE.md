## Chapter 26 — The Money Math

Let us do the math that Plante Moran does not do.

**Revenue:**
PCA receives approximately $8,700 per pupil per year in state
school aid funding. With approximately 400 students, that is
$3.48 million in state funds. Add federal Title I funding
(for high-poverty schools), Title II (for teacher quality),
IDEA (for special education), and other categorical grants.
Total annual revenue: approximately $4.9 million.

**Expenses (as reported):**
72.67% flows to Purpose Group LLC as a "management fee."
That is approximately $3.56 million per year.

What does the management fee cover? According to the school's
financial statements, it covers "management services." That's
it. The disclosure does not itemize what services are provided.
It does not break down the fee into salary, benefits,
facilities, curriculum, technology, professional development,
or any other category.

$3.56 million for "management services." Provided by a single-
member LLC. With no employees. Operated by a man with nine
convictions and a fake law degree.

**Comparison:**
The typical charter management organization (CMO) charges
10-15% of revenue as a management fee. Some charge up to 20%.
The national average, according to the National Alliance for
Public Charter Schools, is approximately 12%.

PCA's management fee: 72.67%.

That is not a management fee. That is the school's budget.

**What's left:**
After the 72.67% extraction, PCA retains approximately 27.33%
of its revenue — roughly $1.34 million — to pay for:
- Teacher salaries (14 staff)
- Facilities (building, utilities, maintenance)
- Food service
- Transportation
- Curriculum materials
- Technology
- Insurance
- Compliance and reporting

$1.34 million for everything. For 400 children. For an entire
school year.

That is $3,350 per child per year after the management fee.

Grosse Pointe Public Schools — where Banks' own children
attend — spends approximately $14,000 per pupil per year.
Detroit Public Schools Community District spends approximately
$15,000 per pupil. PCA, after extraction, spends $3,350.

The children at PCA receive less than one-quarter of the
per-pupil spending of their peers. Not because the funding
isn't there — $8,700 per pupil enters the pipeline. But
72.67% of it exits through the LLC before it reaches a
classroom.

Three percent math proficiency is not a mystery. It is
arithmetic. You cannot educate a child on $3,350 per year when
every other school spends four times that. The money was
there. It was extracted.

The children got the remainder.
