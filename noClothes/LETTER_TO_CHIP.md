## WHAT YOUR DEFENSE TEAM SHOULD KNOW

### 1. The Dismissal Matters

Your 2020 charges were dismissed in March 2023 — by the prosecutors, not by you. They said they would refile. They waited two years and filed different charges. In federal practice, a voluntary dismissal followed by refiling with substantially different allegations raises questions about the strength of the original case and the basis for the new one.

### 2. The Informant Question

Kevin told me his understanding is that an incentivized informant was involved. If a cooperating witness was compensated (reduced charges, financial consideration, immunity) in exchange for testimony, your defense team should aggressively pursue:
- All cooperation agreements (Giglio material)
- All benefits provided to cooperating witnesses (monetary, sentencing, immunity)
- All communications between cooperating witnesses and prosecutors
- Brady material — anything exculpatory

The government's obligation to disclose this material is absolute. *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83 (1963). *Giglio v. United States*, 405 U.S. 150 (1972).

### 3. The Comparative Prosecution Argument

Your defense team may want to consider a selective prosecution argument or, at minimum, introduce the comparative context at sentencing or in motions. The USAO-EDMI's resource allocation decisions are relevant to:
- Whether similarly situated actors (e.g., charter school operators committing fraud in the same district) receive comparable scrutiny
- Whether the prosecution reflects institutional priorities rather than the gravity of the alleged conduct
- The absence of patient harm compared to cases where the same office has declined to investigate demonstrable harm to children

### 4. The Qui Tam / Whistleblower Angle

The 2020 civil suit mentioned Sefi Grossman and Traci Grossman as individual plaintiffs alongside the federal government and state of Michigan. This suggests a qui tam (whistleblower) action under the False Claims Act. Your defense team should know:
- Qui tam relators receive 15-30% of any recovery — they are financially incentivized
- The relationship between the civil qui tam and the criminal prosecution should be examined
- If the same witnesses appear in both proceedings, their financial interest in the outcome is discoverable

### 5. The Scale Defense

304,000 procedures across 26 clinics over 35 years. In any medical practice of that scale, billing disputes are statistically inevitable. The question is whether the alleged conduct represents a systemic fraud scheme or isolated billing errors in a high-volume practice. Your team should:
- Establish the error rate relative to total claims (304,000+ procedures)

- Challenge any extrapolation from a small sample to the full practice
