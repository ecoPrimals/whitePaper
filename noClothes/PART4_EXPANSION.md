## Chapter 13B — The Nassar Pattern

There is a pattern in American institutional abuse.

It goes like this: A credential. A position. An institution.
Complaints. Silence. More complaints. More silence. Years.
Decades. Then — suddenly, violently — it pops.

Larry Nassar abused gymnasts for twenty-three years. From
1994 to 2017. At Michigan State University. At USA Gymnastics.
At the Karolyi Ranch in Texas.

People complained. Rachael Denhollander filed a report in 2004.
MSU investigated and cleared Nassar. Amanda Thomashow filed a
complaint in 2014. MSU investigated and cleared Nassar again.

MSU — a flagship state university with a $700 million endowment,
a legal department, a Title IX office, a police department,
and 50,000 students — investigated a serial predator TWICE
and cleared him TWICE.

Then it popped. The Indianapolis Star published. The dam broke.
Over 500 victims came forward. Nassar received 175 years. MSU
paid $500 million in settlements. The university president
resigned. The athletic director was charged. The dean was
charged. The Olympic committee was restructured.

Here is the pattern:

1. **The Credential**: Nassar was "Dr. Nassar." A medical
   degree. Banks is "Dr. Banks, J.D., Ph.D." Three credentials,
   one of them fabricated.

2. **The Institution**: MSU employed Nassar. DPSCD authorized
   Banks. Both institutions had the power to stop it and didn't.

3. **The Complaints**: Denhollander complained. Thomashow
   complained. In Banks' case: parents complained. The
   Michigan Department of Education received reports. The AGC
   received grievances about the false J.D. Nothing happened.

4. **The Clearing**: MSU investigated and cleared Nassar.
   DPSCD authorized and reauthorized Banks' charter. MDE
   issued no adverse action. Same pattern: investigate the
   complaint, clear the subject, close the file.

5. **The Pop**: For Nassar, it was the Indianapolis Star. For
   Banks, we don't know yet. This book may be the Star. The
   site may be. A journalist may find it independently. The
   969 people who cloned the repository on October 12, 2026,
   may include the reporter who writes the story.

6. **The Aftermath**: When Nassar popped, EVERYONE fell. Not
   just Nassar. The president, the AD, the dean, the board
   members, the Olympic officials. When it pops — and it
   always pops — everyone connected falls.

Look at the board of Purpose Charter Academy. A judge with
three scandals. An AG employee who sits on a felon's board.
A city council member who oversees the overseer. A five-time
bankrupt drug convict controlling the money.

When it pops, they all fall. Every one of them. That is the
pattern. It has never failed. The only variable is time.

The difference between Nassar and Banks is scale of harm and
type of harm. Nassar's harm was physical. Banks' harm is
educational — the slow-motion destruction of children's
futures through systematic deprivation. 3% math. Uncertified
teachers. Millions extracted.

Both are credential-enabled. Both are institution-protected.
Both have complaint histories that were absorbed by the
membrane and never transmitted.

And both will pop.

## Chapter 14B — The Tuskegee Inversion

From 1932 to 1972, the United States Public Health Service
conducted a study on 399 Black men in Tuskegee, Alabama.

The men had syphilis. They were told they were being treated.
They were not. The USPHS watched them die — over forty years
— to document the progression of untreated syphilis. The men
were not given penicillin when it became available in 1947.
They were not told they were in an experiment. Their families
were not told. Their doctors were not told.

The Tuskegee Syphilis Study is the defining case of
institutional betrayal in American medicine. It destroyed
trust between Black communities and healthcare institutions —
trust that has never fully recovered, as vaccination
hesitancy data during COVID-19 confirms.

But there is a detail that the public narrative omits.

The study was conducted at the **Tuskegee Institute** — now
Tuskegee University. The institution founded by **Booker T.
Washington**. The home of the **Tuskegee Airmen** — the Black
military aviators who broke barriers in World War II. The
institution that represented, to the entire nation, the
possibility of Black excellence against impossible odds.

The USPHS did not choose Tuskegee by accident. They chose it
because the name had credibility. Because Tuskegee meant
something to Black families. Because the airmen had earned
trust that the experimenters could spend.

This is the Tuskegee Inversion: using the legacy of Black
excellence as the cover for Black exploitation.

Brian Banks does this.

His book is called "The Agony of Success." His school is called
"Purpose." His credential — the J.D. he doesn't hold — is a
signal to Black parents that here is a Black man who climbed
out. Who beat the system. Who went to law school despite his
record. Who came back to build something.

The credential is the legacy. The school is the institution.
The narrative — redemption, transformation, purpose — is
Tuskegee's name on the door.

Behind the name: 3% math. Uncertified teachers. $4.9 million
to his LLC. Five-time bankrupt treasurer. BMF lineage. Captured
judges. And four hundred children who will graduate — if they
graduate — unable to compete with children from schools where
the teachers have credentials and the superintendent doesn't
have nine convictions.

The Tuskegee researchers studied the progression of untreated
disease.

Brian Banks is studying the progression of untreated ignorance.

The disease is the same: a system that profits from the harm
it inflicts on the community that trusts it.

The airmen built Tuskegee. Banks is breaking what they built.

## Chapter 15C — The 5,432

We count the children.

Not as a statistic. As a debt.

Brian Banks has operated schools since approximately 2014.
Purpose Charter Academy enrolls approximately 400 students
per year. MacDowell Preparatory Academy — the second school —
enrolls an additional cohort. Over the years, the total number
of child-years — one child, one year of education — exceeds
five thousand.

5,432 child-years. Approximately.

Each child-year is a child who spent a year in a building
where three percent of students are proficient in math. A
year where the teacher probably (85.7% probability) was not
certified by the state. A year where 72.67% of the funding
went to one man's LLC.

Some of those children spent multiple years. A child who
entered PCA in kindergarten and stayed through eighth grade
spent nine years — nine child-years — inside the membrane.
That child will enter high school at a ninth-grade reading
level on paper, but a fourth-grade reading level in practice.

The gap compounds.

A child who is behind in third grade has a 75% chance of
remaining behind in ninth grade (Annie E. Casey Foundation,
2010). A child who is behind in ninth grade has a 50% chance
of not graduating (National Center for Education Statistics).
A child who does not graduate earns, on average, $30,000 less
per year than a high school graduate (Bureau of Labor
Statistics).

Over a 40-year career, that gap is $1.2 million per child.

Multiply by the number of children who passed through Banks'
schools unable to read or do math. Conservatively: 300
children over ten years. (Not all 400 per year, and not all
ten years at maximum enrollment.)

$1.2 million × 300 = $360 million in lifetime earnings lost.

Brian Banks extracted approximately $4.9 million per year ×
10 years = $49 million.

He extracted $49 million. The children lost $360 million.

That is the exchange rate. For every dollar Banks took, the
children lost seven.
